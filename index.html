ANALGESICS - FULL GUIDE UNTAMPERED


SOURCE: Analgesics_Pharmaceutical_Reference_Guide.docx - 2026 EDITION
IMPORTANT: Educational only - not a substitute for prescription, label, guideline, or pharmacist/doctor assessment.

1. Executive overview

Analgesics are medicines used to relieve pain. They include non-opioid analgesics such as paracetamol, NSAIDs, and opioid analgesics. Paracetamol mainly analgesic and antipyretic; little anti-inflammatory. NSAIDs provide analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory by inhibiting COX. Opioids act at opioid receptors reserved for moderate-to-severe pain because of sedation, respiratory depression, dependence.

4. Paracetamol details

Adult 500mg: 1-2 tablets per dose at least 4h apart; max 8x500mg/24h. 1g: one tablet per dose, max four 1g tablets/24h. Do not combine multiple paracetamol-containing products. Liver disease, low body weight, heavy alcohol, malnutrition require lower dose. Overdose can cause severe liver injury fatal. FDA: max 4000mg/day adults 12+.

5. NSAIDs

Use lowest effective dose shortest duration. Avoid two systemic NSAIDs together. Risks: dyspepsia, gastritis, ulcer, GI bleeding, renal impairment, fluid retention, CV events. Caution elderly, kidney disease, HF, HTN, ulcer history, anticoagulant use. Interactions: anticoagulants, corticosteroids, antidepressants, antihypertensives, diuretics. Pregnancy requires review; generally avoided unless advised.

6. Aspirin

Higher doses pain relief, low doses antiplatelet. Pain: 300mg 1-3 tablets per dose at least 4h apart max 4 doses/day. Low-dose 75mg OD not stronger painkiller; antiplatelet only. Do not start/stop low-dose without advice. Do not give to children under 16 unless prescribed.

7. Opioids

Reserved for selected moderate-severe pain. Dosing individualized. IMPORTANT: Never provide generic opioid dose as universal. Requires assessment, monitoring, interaction review, secure storage.

MedicineKey pointRisks
MorphineStrong opioid titrated individuallyRespiratory depression, sedation, hypotension, nausea, constipation
TramadolMonoaminergic activitySeizures, serotonin syndrome, sedation, dependence

8-11. Prescription, Expiry, Storage

Prescription must have patient, medicine, strength, form, dose, route, frequency, duration, prescriber per law. Check allergies, age, pregnancy, renal/hepatic, current meds, duplicate ingredients. Check OTC/pharmacy/Rx/controlled Uganda framework. Expiry EXP is manufacturer limit unopened. Beyond-use date after opened/reconstituted. Never use after expiry. Do not guess expiry from manufacturing date. Reconstituted liquids shorter BUD write date. Do not use if appearance deteriorated. Expiry not universal years must read package. This guide does not invent expiry dates.

12-15. Interactions, Counseling, Red flags

Paracetamol + cold/flu product → overdose. Ibuprofen + naproxen/aspirin → duplication. NSAID + anticoagulant → bleeding. NSAID + corticosteroid → GI injury. Tramadol + serotonergic antidepressants → serotonin syndrome. Tramadol/opioid + alcohol/sedatives → CNS/respiratory depression. Always check active ingredients not brand. Counseling: confirm identity, pain, severity, duration, red-flags, allergies, meds, supplements, herbal. Explain dose, interval, route, max daily, food, duplicate warning, adverse effects, urgent help, storage away children, teach-back. Red flags: severe/sudden pain, chest pain, severe abdominal/headache, trauma/fracture, persistent fever, worsening pain, vomiting blood, black stools GI bleeding, breathing difficulty, facial swelling, rash anaphylaxis, drowsiness slow breathing inability wake after opioid, overdose paracetamol/opioids, new neuro symptoms, weakness, confusion.

16-19. Safety rules

Never exceed labeled dose. Never assume two brands different actives. Do not combine systemic NSAIDs unless instructed. Keep opioids secure never share. Check expiry/storage. Use exact measuring device liquids. Ask pharmacist/doctor when pregnancy, chronic disease, multiple medicines, severe pain or overdose. Dispose expired via pharmacy/waste pathway.
