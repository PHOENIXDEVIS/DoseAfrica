Understanding Anticancer Therapy

Anticancer medicines are drugs used to treat malignant diseases. They may act by damaging DNA, blocking cell division, interfering with hormone signalling, inhibiting specific molecular targets, stimulating the immune system, or delivering toxic substances selectively to cancer cells.


Cancer treatment is highly individualised. The selected medicine, dose, treatment cycle and combination depend on the cancer type, stage, molecular characteristics, organ function, previous therapy, patient factors and treatment protocol.

Important clinical safety notice: The doses shown in this educational database are NOT universal prescriptions. Many anticancer medicines require specialist oncology protocols, body-surface-area or weight-based calculations, renal/hepatic adjustment, laboratory monitoring and combination regimens. Patients should never start, stop or change anticancer therapy without their oncology/medical team.
